# The Long Way Around — Book Outline

**Title:** The Long Way Around
**Subtitle:** Building a Business, Phase by Phase
**Author:** Wouter Simons, founder and CEO of Lionsville

A generic book about being an entrepreneur. It is not the story of Lionsville or any
one company. Written directly to the reader, casual humor, active voice, invented
composite stories for emotional connection.

**Target length:** roughly 500 pages (~155,000 words). Introduction ~4,000 words,
each chapter 9,000–10,000 words.

---

## Recurring cast (invented composites, consistent across chapters)

- **Henk** — Wouter's first mentor. Retired machine-parts wholesaler. Blunt
  one-liners, smells faintly of cigars, annoyingly right. Appears in intro, ch 2, 6, 12, 15.
- **Marta** — friend of Wouter, founded a design agency that grew to about 40 people.
  Learned branding and hiring the hard way. Appears in ch 1, 8, 10, 11.
- **Jonas** — Wouter's brother-in-law. Turned one bakery into nine. Practical,
  allergic to jargon. Appears in ch 4, 6, 13, 14.
- **Priya** — former colleague, founder of a B2B software company, raised money
  once, regretted parts of it. Appears in ch 3, 5, 6, 9, 15.
- **Sam** — Wouter's cofounder in his first failed venture (a recipe app that died
  at 22). Still friends. Appears in ch 5, 7, 16.
- **Wouter's own invented arc:** built websites for local shops at 19; recipe app
  failure with Sam at 22; then "the routing company" (logistics software) which he
  grew and eventually sold; now Lionsville. Referenced lightly, never as the plot.

The introduction carries an author's note: all stories are true the way bar stories
are true. Composites, names changed, details rearranged to protect the guilty.

## Structure

### Front matter
- **00 — Introduction: The Long Way Around** (~4,000 words)
  Why phases matter, why most business books lie by tidiness, who this book is for,
  how to read it, the author's note about stories, the map of the sixteen phases.
  Visual: SVG map of the 16 phases as a winding road.

### Part I — Who You Are Before What You Sell
1. **Designing an Identity** — who the company is, before mission or product.
   Values you actually hold vs. poster values. Founder identity vs. company identity.
   Marta's agency identity crisis. Table: real values vs. decorative values.
   SVG: identity as the trunk, mission/vision/brand as branches.
   NOT covered here: mission statements (ch 2), vision (ch 3), logos/brand (ch 8).
2. **Deciding on Mission** — the job the company does in the world. How to write a
   mission you can use to say no. Henk's "what do people pay you to make go away?"
   Table: mission smell test. NOT: vision imagery (ch 3), OKR mechanics (ch 13).
3. **Articulating Vision** — the postcard from the future. Vision vs. mission vs.
   strategy in one table, told once here and never re-explained elsewhere.
   Priya's whiteboard story. SVG: horizon diagram (now → 3y → 10y).
   NOT: planning mechanics (ch 7), goal cascades (ch 13).
4. **Building the Product or Service Strategy** — what you sell, to whom, why they
   care. Problem-first thinking, positioning, pricing basics, the painful art of
   saying no to features. Jonas's croissant line. Tables: strategy one-pager,
   pricing approaches. SVG: value proposition fit diagram.
   NOT: sales process (ch 9), brand expression (ch 8).

### Part II — People and Money
5. **Getting the Founding Team Together** — cofounders, equity, roles, the
   prenup conversation. Sam and the recipe app postmortem. Priya's solo regret.
   Tables: cofounder question list, equity split factors. SVG: skills/temperament map.
   NOT: employee hiring (ch 10), org design (ch 11).
6. **Managing Money** — cashflow vs. profit, liquidity, reading a P&L without
   crying, self-funding vs. investment, runway math. Jonas's flour invoice crisis.
   Henk's "profit is opinion, cash is fact." Tables: P&L walkthrough, funding
   options compared, 13-week cashflow template. SVG: cash gap timeline.
   NOT: valuation at exit (ch 15), scaling finance ops (ch 14).
7. **Evolving the Business Plan** — the plan as a living document, not a 40-page
   PDF. Assumptions, kill criteria, pivots. Recipe app: the plan they never updated.
   Tables: one-page plan, assumption tracker. SVG: plan-learn-adjust loop.
   NOT: pitch decks (touched in ch 6), EOS cadence (ch 13).

### Part III — Going to Market
8. **Building the Brand** — brand as reputation, not logo. Naming, voice, promise,
   consistency. Marta's rebrand disaster and recovery. Tables: brand promise
   worksheet, touchpoint audit. SVG: brand = what they say when you leave the room.
   NOT: company identity/values (ch 1), sales messaging tactics (ch 9).
9. **Sales Approach** — founder-led selling, discovery, pipelines that fit your
   business, pricing conversations, losing gracefully. Priya's first enterprise
   deal. Tables: sales motion picker, discovery questions, pipeline stages.
   SVG: pipeline funnel with honest leak labels. NOT: marketing/brand (ch 8),
   sales team scaling (ch 14).
10. **Hiring People** — first hires, hiring for the company you are, interviews
    that predict, onboarding, letting go kindly. Marta's brilliant-jerk story.
    Tables: scorecard template, first-ten-hires sequencing. SVG: hiring funnel.
    NOT: founding team (ch 5), org structure (ch 11), culture design (ch 1).

### Part IV — Building the Machine
11. **Building the Skeleton and Nervous System as You Grow** — structure (skeleton)
    and information flow (nervous system): roles, meetings, documentation, feedback
    loops, metrics that carry signal. Marta at 25 people: "nobody tells me anything
    anymore." Tables: meeting audit, signal vs. noise metrics. SVG: skeleton/nervous
    system anatomy metaphor. NOT: EOS specifics (ch 13), leadership skills (ch 12).
12. **Evolving Your Own Management and Leadership Skills** — the founder as
    bottleneck, delegation, feedback, self-awareness, energy management, getting
    coached. Henk's mirror speech. Tables: delegation ladder, founder failure modes.
    SVG: what-got-you-here curve. NOT: org mechanics (ch 11), EOS roles (ch 13).
13. **The Entrepreneurial Operating System** — operating rhythms and frameworks
    (EOS and cousins): vision alignment, data, issues, process, traction. When a
    framework helps and when it becomes theater. Jonas runs L10 meetings in a
    bakery. Tables: EOS components mapped, framework comparison (EOS, OKR, 4DX).
    SVG: operating cadence wheel (annual/quarterly/weekly/daily).
    NOT: re-explaining vision/mission (ch 2–3), generic meeting hygiene (ch 11).
14. **Scaling Up** — when to scale, what breaks (people, process, cash, founder),
    scaling the sales engine, internationalization, keeping the soul. Jonas's ninth
    bakery, the one that nearly sank the other eight. Tables: scale readiness
    checklist, what-breaks-at-what-size. SVG: growth stair-step (not a hockey stick).
    NOT: fundraising basics (ch 6), org design basics (ch 11).

### Part V — Endings and Beginnings
15. **Selling the Business** — why founders sell, valuation reality, finding buyers,
    diligence, earn-outs, the emotional cliff after closing. Wouter selling the
    routing company; Henk's "you are not the company." Priya's almost-sale.
    Tables: exit types compared, diligence checklist, deal terms glossary.
    SVG: deal timeline. NOT: startup funding (ch 6).
16. **Doing It Again** — serial entrepreneurship, what transfers and what doesn't,
    the ego trap of the second company, angel investing vs. building, rest,
    and why you will probably do it again anyway. Sam's second act. Closing letter
    to the reader. Table: first time vs. second time. SVG: the road loops back.
    Includes short epilogue.

## Per-chapter required elements

Every chapter contains:
- An opening story (cold open, no throat-clearing)
- 7–10 sections with h2 headings, h3 subsections where useful
- 2–3 `.story` blocks (invented anecdotes, using assigned cast)
- 2–4 tables (real, usable content, not filler)
- 1–2 inline SVG diagrams in `figure.diagram`
- 1–2 `.try-this` exercise blocks
- 1 `.pullquote`
- Optional `.callout.warn` / `.callout.note` blocks
- A closing `.short-version` block titled "The Short Version" with 6–10 bullets
- `nav.chapter-nav` with prev / contents / next links
